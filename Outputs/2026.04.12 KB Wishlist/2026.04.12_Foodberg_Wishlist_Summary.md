## Purpose

Foodberg currently ships ~316K price records from USDA WASDE, FRED, BLS, FAO, and the World Bank, with zero accompanying scholarly literature explaining *why* food prices move the way they do. This wishlist closes that gap. Downstream, these documents will feed the HDARP pipeline (`/preparehdarp` → `/sphdarp`) and integrate into a Foodberg knowledge base that lets the app present historical context alongside numerical charts.

## Top 10 "Must Acquire" (CRITICAL priority, highest relevance)

1. **Gardner, Bruce** — *American Agriculture in the Twentieth Century* (2002) — definitive US ag-policy history.
2. **Cochrane, Willard** — *The Development of American Agriculture* (1993) — companion volume; the "treadmill" framework.
3. **Cronon, William** — *Nature's Metropolis: Chicago and the Great West* (1991) — how CBOT and grain markets were built.
4. **Headey & Fan** — "Anatomy of a Crisis" (2008) — canonical explanation of the 2007–08 food spike.
5. **Anderson, Kym** — *Distortions to Agricultural Incentives* (2009, World Bank) — defining analysis of global trade-policy price effects.
6. **Sen, Amartya** — *Poverty and Famines* (1981) — Nobel-grade framework on prices and entitlement.
7. **Evenson & Gollin** — "Assessing the Impact of the Green Revolution" (Science, 2003) — the secular productivity/price story.
8. **MacDonald, James** — *Consolidation in U.S. Meatpacking* (2000) — why 4 firms dominate US beef.
9. **Carter, Rausser, Smith** — "The Effect of the U.S. Ethanol Mandate on Corn Prices" (2017) — the RFS-price link.
10. **FAO** — *FAO Food Price Index Technical Note* (2020) — methodology of the very index Foodberg displays.

## Gap Analysis

Things this wishlist **deliberately under-covers** and future iterations should expand:

1. **Non-English-language scholarship** (especially Chinese & Spanish-language commodity literatures) — only English entries included here.
2. **Pre-1900 price histories outside the Anglo world** — Mintz covers sugar, but Indian, Chinese, and sub-Saharan price histories are thin.
3. **Commodity-trader archives** — Cargill, ADM, Bunge, Dreyfus (ABCD) internal publications are not generally catalogable but would enrich Category 4.
4. **Microdata studies** — IRI/Nielsen retail scanner data analyses of food CPI were mostly omitted (licensing constraints).
5. **Restaurant-industry econometric studies** — Category 13 is thinner than ideal; foodservice econ as a research field is small.
6. **Crypto / commodity-ETF financialization literature** — lightly covered (Irwin/Sanders papers included); more recent 2022+ work exists.
