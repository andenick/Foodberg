## 1. Context — Why NYC, Why Now

Foodberg ships national USDA/FAO data. But **most Foodberg users will be NYC-adjacent chefs** (East Coast fine-dining axis, Culinary Institute graduates, NYC restaurant staff). For them, the national wheat-price chart is less immediately useful than knowing:

- Why Hunts Point sets the morning price for 22 million people.
- Why a NYC restaurant's food cost runs 32–35% vs. a national 28–30% average.
- How Seamless → Grubhub → DoorDash reshaped delivery-commission economics in the city that birthed internet food delivery.
- Why bodegas — a quasi-industry of 15,000+ stores — persist despite Walmart's suburban dominance.
- How the 2020 outdoor-dining revolution made NYC a live laboratory for restaurant reform.
- How immigration waves (Jewish, Italian, Chinese, Korean, Mexican, Bangladeshi, West African) each built pricing, distribution, and labor sub-systems that national datasets render invisible.

v1 and v2 did not even attempt NYC coverage — a gap so large it justifies a dedicated wishlist branch. v3 addresses it comprehensively, treating NYC not as a case study but as its own food system with distinct sub-industries and a distinct historiography.

**Intended outcome**: the single best catalog of scholarly, institutional, archival, trade-press, and primary-source references for understanding NYC's food economy, past and present.

- **Individual restaurateur memoirs beyond canonical 5–10** — Meyer, Reichl, Bourdain, Keller, Ripert, Boulud — include selectively; full memoirs collection deferred.

- **Cookbook as primary source** — all five-borough-specific cookbooks (Smitten Kitchen, Momofuku, Ivan Ramen) are out of scope.

## 15. Key Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| NYPL menu collection is huge; risk of drowning | Hard-cap at 30 NYPL entries; curate by restaurant historical significance |
| NYC Municipal Archives requires physical visit for much material | Catalog reference pointers; mark `Status=UNAVAILABLE_NEEDS_VISIT`; focus on digitized fraction |
| Paywalled Crain's / Real Deal / REBNY reports | Use as citations; don't expect acquisition; note `Status=PARTIAL` |
| Journalist pieces proliferate — how to limit | Scholarly or institutional reports get CSV rows; ephemeral journalism only gets cited in companion summary |
| Over-reliance on a few prolific scholars (Smith, Ray, Diner) | Enforce author-cap (max 6 works per author) |
| v3 drifts beyond NYC into generic US urban-food scholarship | Strict gate: every entry must have NYC relevance either (a) about NYC directly, (b) NYC as case study, or (c) NYC metro data source |
